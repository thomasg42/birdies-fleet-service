Mobile mechanic in Big Sky

J comes to the vehicle. Send the place it is parked.

Book a time Call 435-531-4127

Big Sky stretches along US-191 in Gallatin Canyon, with the Gallatin River beside the highway and Lone Peak above the resort and the construction. The community sits in both Gallatin County and Madison County, which is a map fact and a directions fact: people will name either county and mean the same mountain town.

Construction at the resort and along the canyon puts equipment and pickups on pads that do not have a mailbox yet. A crew cab at a footing, a loader beside a material pile, and a diesel left at a trailhead parking lot are three different pins. The booking note should pick one and describe the vehicle, not "somewhere in Big Sky."

Canyon grades punish cooling systems and brakes. A gauge that climbed between the turnoff and the resort, or a grind that started on the way down, is the sentence J needs. Hunting-season and ski-season traffic changes how long a pullout stays safe. If the truck is still on the highway shoulder, say that first so the meeting place is obvious.

Lone Peak's weather is not the valley's weather. A battery that was fine in Belgrade can be silent after a night at altitude. Mention the night it sat out. Ranch and guest-ranch equipment on the Madison County side of the hill belongs in the note as equipment, with the gate code or the person who will open it.

Forest roads leaving the canyon are backcountry calls. Use a pin plus the last named trailhead you passed. Pavement inside the resort core can use a building name. Both are valid. They should not be mixed into one vague sentence.

A Big Sky job is not a Bozeman driveway. The distance and the canyon are the point. Call or text when the board's next public day is too late.
