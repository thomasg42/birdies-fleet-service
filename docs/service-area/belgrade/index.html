Belgrade sits in Gallatin County on Interstate 90, with Jackrabbit Lane as the commercial strip people actually name when they give directions. Bozeman Yellowstone International Airport occupies the east side, so loops, curb lanes, and service roads see a steady mix of vans, crew cabs, and fuel trucks.

Yards here are often gravel behind a metal building, not a numbered downtown stall. A contractor stacks pickups at a laydown off the frontage. A shuttle van waits on the airport road between runs. A farm truck from the bench parks at the edge of a field approach west of the strip. Those are different meeting places, and the booking note should say which one.

Jackrabbit traffic means a lot of short trips and a lot of idling. Charging complaints and brake complaints both show up after that kind of week. The airport loop adds another version: a vehicle that has to be back on a schedule and cannot spare the drive to a bay across the valley. Meeting it beside the lot saves that drive.

The bench north and west of town is open and windy. A diesel left there overnight is a cold-start story. Write down whether it clicked, cranked slowly, or did nothing. Wind also hides a small coolant leak until the puddle blows dry, so mention a smell or a temperature climb if that is what the driver saw.

Directions in Belgrade go bad when someone says "by the airport" and means three different roads. Name Jackrabbit, the frontage, or the field approach. If the lot has no sign, drop a pin. Fleet units should include the unit number and which row of the yard.

This town is its own page because the airport and Jackrabbit Lane are not the same problem as a canyon pullout or a river ranch. Book the window for the place the vehicle will actually be sitting.
