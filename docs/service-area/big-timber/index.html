Big Timber is the Sweet Grass County seat on Interstate 90. The Boulder River road runs south into the mountains, and the Crazy Mountains stand off to the north. The town is a small grid with a loud highway and a lot of ranch country beyond the last block.

Meeting places split cleanly. Frontage and in-town yards are ordinary addresses. The Boulder River road is a marker-and-pin problem once the pavement narrows. Ranch roads north toward the Crazies are gate-and-person problems. Say which of the three you mean in the first sentence so the window is spent driving to the right one.

Ranch diesels and pickups here work dusty section roads and then sit in the weather. A wander after a washboard stretch, a hum that tracks speed, or a hydraulic leak at the shop yard are the kinds of notes that match the card. Write the symptom in driver language. Leave the part name blank if you are guessing.

I-90 brings trucks that would rather not backtrack to a larger city for a no-crank or a brake grind. If the truck is on a ramp or a frontage lot, say the exit you can see. If it is still on the shoulder, say that before anything else. A shoulder is a safety fact, not a color detail.

Wind off the Crazies will strip heat out of an engine bay overnight and will dry a coolant drip by morning. Mention the gauge or the smell if the puddle is gone. A cold no-crank after a clear night is its own sentence: click, slow, or silent.

Big Timber is the east end of the town list. It is not a Livingston overflow page. The Boulder, the Crazies, and the county seat are the landmarks worth typing into the note. Add the exit or the river-road marker before you add anything else.
