Mobile mechanic in Manhattan

J comes to the vehicle. Send the place it is parked.

Manhattan is a Gallatin County town on Interstate 90, in farm country west of Belgrade. The grid is small. The approaches around it are not. Grain trucks, flatbeds, and family pickups share the same few streets, then scatter onto section roads that do not look like streets at all.

A vehicle in Manhattan might be beside a shop on the main road, at a bin site during harvest, or at the end of a farm lane with the gate shut. Say whether the gate is shut and who will open it. The lane name, if it has one, belongs in the first line of the note.

Farm use shows up as loaded suspension, dusty brakes, and diesels that idle beside a pile for a long time. A hum that started when the truck was heavy is a bearing clue. A miss that only happens under load is a diesel clue. Write the condition, not a parts guess.

Wind across the bench between here and the river will hide a drip. If the driver smelled coolant or watched the gauge, that sentence is the whole diagnosis you should attempt. J does the rest at the truck.

Because the town is compact, people from Belgrade and Three Forks sometimes treat it as a pass-through. If the truck is actually on a Manhattan approach, book it as Manhattan and describe the approach. A pin beats "west of Belgrade" when the board is trying to show a window.

Harvest changes the yards for a few weeks. If the unit will be at a different bin tomorrow, say where it will be during the window you pick, not where it was this morning. A bin number or a road approach is the line that keeps the visit on the right farm. Repeat the approach if the bin and the house do not share a road.
