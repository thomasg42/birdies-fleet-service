Mobile mechanic in Ennis

Jay comes to the vehicle. Send the place it is parked.

Ennis is in Madison County on US-287, with the Madison River as the main landmark and ranch country on both sides of the valley. A Madison Valley pin should not be described as if it were the Gallatin Valley.

Main street is short. The ranches are not. A truck may be at a cafe lot, at a tackle shop pull-in, or eight minutes up a ranch road with the river on the right. Those are different jobs. The ranch road needs a gate person and a pin. The cafe lot needs the side of the building and the vehicle color only if two trucks match.

River roads and fishing access sites create a steady set of "no street address" stops. Hunters and anglers text from spots that have a site number or only a bend in the river. Send the access name if you know it. Send the pin either way. Add what the vehicle is doing: no crank, a grind, a gauge in the red, a wander on the washboard.

Ranch equipment in this valley includes tractors and diesels that live outside all year. Cold starts and hydraulic leaks at the shop yard are yard visits. A machine stuck off a two-track is a backcountry note, and it should say so, because reach is something Jay decides from the description rather than from a web paragraph.

US-287 north leads toward Three Forks. South and west, the valley opens into more ranch roads and the routes people use toward Big Sky's back side. If you are between towns, pick the pin over the town pride. The booking can still start from Ennis.

Wind in the Madison Valley dries a leak and carries sound. If you heard a hum, say the speed it happened. If you saw a temperature climb on the grade out of the canyon, say the grade. Specific notes replace a second trip.
