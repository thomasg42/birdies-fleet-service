Southwest Montana

Backcountry repair

On-site repair for Forest Service roads, trailheads, campsites, and hunting areas in Southwest Montana. Text J your pin. Call 435-531-4127.

Book a time Call 435-531-4127

Backcountry repair is for vehicles that are already off the pavement. The card lists Forest Service roads, trailheads, campsites, hunting areas, recovery assistance, and on-site repair. J Smith comes to the vehicle. Not every road is reachable, a phone may have no signal, and the visit follows the note you send.

What to send is specific. The nearest road or trail. A mile marker if you can see one. A GPS pin. The vehicle. What it is doing. That is enough for J to decide whether he can meet you there. Text J your location puts the pin into a text on your own phone. The pin stays in that text.

If the phone cannot get a location, the button still opens a text and asks you to add the nearest road, trailhead, or mile marker. An iPhone Compass reading can help. In Google Maps, a long-press drops a pin you can paste. Those are instructions for your phone, not a claim that coverage exists on the ridge.

Forest Service roads and trailheads are meeting places, not a list of roads J has graded or patrols. Campsites and hunting areas are the same. Name the drainage or the sign you can see. If you are not sure of the official name, send the pin and describe the last pavement you left.

On-site repair means the work he can do at the vehicle: the mobile repair list, a diesel that will not start, a coolant leak, a belt, a battery. If the job needs a shop, J will say so. Recovery assistance is on the card. It is not a promise of a winch, a trailer, or a pull. Ask him what he can do with the note you send. Hauling the vehicle somewhere else is not the offer.

Today or tomorrow is a phone call or a text. The schedule page is for later windows, first come, first served, in Mountain Time. A public booking cannot be made for today. If you are stuck now, call 435-531-4127.

Ask J for a quote once he knows the vehicle and the spot. Nothing here invents a rescue story. The useful thing is the text with the pin and a clear description of the fault.

Town pages cover Bozeman, Belgrade, Big Sky, Livingston, Manhattan, Three Forks, Ennis, West Yellowstone, Gardiner, and Big Timber. Canyons and river roads out of those towns are where these calls start. Use the town page if you want the local roads, then come back here for the location text.

Send one text, not three fragments. Pin, vehicle, and the fault should land together. If the phone drops signal after the pin loads, that single text is the one that matters. Add a mile marker when you can read one, and name the last pavement you left. J uses that to decide if the spot is a place he can meet you. J decides from that text.

On-site repair still means the mobile list: brakes, a battery, a starter, coolant, a belt, a diesel that quit. If the vehicle is off a trail and will not roll, say that in the first line. Recovery assistance stays a question for the call. Not every spur road is in reach.

If you can see a sign, read it into the text. If you can see a mile marker, read the number. If you only know the last town you drove through, say that town and the direction you left it. One complete text beats a string of partial ones sent as the signal fades.

If a second vehicle is with you, say so, and say which one will not move. A pin for the one that is stopped is the pin that matters. Call or text 435-531-4127 when you need J before the next public window.
