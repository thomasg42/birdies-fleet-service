Fleet maintenance

Preventive maintenance, diagnosis, and emergency fleet repair where the trucks already sit. J Smith comes to the yard. Call or text 435-531-4127.

Book a time Call 435-531-4127

Fleet maintenance is the work that keeps a group of vehicles moving without hauling every unit to a bay. Preventive maintenance, diagnosis, and emergency fleet repair are the items on J Smith's card. DOT inspections are listed with them. Ask J what the look includes when you book.

Preventive maintenance is the planned visit. Oil, filters, and the small checks a fleet already tracks can happen in the yard where the trucks park at the end of a shift. You tell J which unit, what is due, and where it will be sitting. He comes to that spot. The public schedule opens after tomorrow. A fleet account can ask for a sooner window.

Diagnosis starts with what the driver felt. A miss, a warning lamp, a hard start, a pull, or a noise that showed up on the grade. Write that down in the booking note. The more specific the note, the less time gets spent guessing in the yard. Diagnosis on site means the truck does not have to be trailered before anyone knows what failed.

Emergency fleet repair is the unplanned one. A unit will not start, will not steer, or cannot finish the loop it was assigned. Call or text J for today or tomorrow. The online board is for later windows, first come, first served. If you have a fleet PIN, sign in and the board will show which windows are open, which are already taken by a customer, and which are held.

Bring the unit number, the yard or job site, and a person who can point at the truck. If the yard has a gate, say so. If the truck is on a ranch lane past the cattle guard, say that too. A pin from your phone helps when the address is a field name nobody else uses.

Ask J for a quote, and ask how soon he can reach the yard. The card is not a parts counter. The offer is the visit: J comes to the fleet.

Towns on the schedule include Bozeman, Belgrade, Manhattan, Three Forks, Ennis, Big Sky, West Yellowstone, Gardiner, Livingston, and Big Timber. The roads change. The booking note stays the same: service, unit, place, and a phone number that reaches the person standing next to the truck.

If you are setting up several units, use the contact page and choose Set up a fleet account. J will sort the PIN with you. Until that account exists, book a single window on the schedule or text the yard details directly.

A useful fleet note names the unit, the row in the yard, and the person who will be standing there. If two trucks look alike, the unit number is the difference that matters. If a lamp is on, say which lamp. If the truck still drives, say whether it should be left running. J should not have to walk the yard to learn a fact you already have. Use the same plain pattern every time you book, even when the yards start to feel familiar.

Windows on the public board are labeled in Mountain Time, morning through afternoon, and they fill in order. The public line does not rank one fleet above another. First come, first served is the public rule. A PIN changes who can ask for a sooner window. It does not change the fields in the note: service, unit, place, and a phone that reaches the person beside the truck.

If the yard locks at dusk, say when someone can still open it. If the unit is on a trailer, say whether it can come off before the window. If two faults are fighting for attention, mark the one that keeps the truck from leaving. The board only stores the window. The note has to carry the rest, every time, in plain language a driver would actually say out loud.
