Mobile repair

Brakes, suspension, steering, bearings, batteries, alternators, starters, and coolant, repaired where the vehicle sits. Call or text 435-531-4127.

Book a time Call 435-531-4127

Mobile repair is the card list Jay Smith actually printed: brakes, suspension, steering, wheel bearings, batteries, alternators, starters, and coolant. The visit happens at the driveway, the shop yard, the job site, the ranch, or the trailhead. You do not have to get the vehicle to a bay first.

Brakes

Grinding, a pull to one side, a soft pedal, or a squeal that showed up on a grade are the notes that help. Say which axle you think is talking, and whether the noise is there every stop or only when the truck is loaded. Jay comes to the vehicle and checks it there. Ask Jay for a quote.

Suspension

Clunks over a cattle guard, a lean, or a bounce that will not settle belong here. Note whether the sound is in the front or the rear, and whether it started after a rough road. A ranch lane and a canyon highway beat suspension in different ways. The booking note should say where the vehicle has been sitting and what the driver felt.

Steering

Pulling and wandering are the two words drivers use most. A truck that drifts in a straight lane, or that needs a constant hand on the wheel, needs a look at the steering before the next long drive. Mention if it got worse after a hit on a rock or a curb. Do not keep driving a wander while you wait on a guess from a distance.

Wheel bearings

A humming that changes with speed, and that gets louder in a gentle turn, is the usual bearing complaint. Say which side you think it is, and whether the sound is new. Wheel bearings are on the card so the work can be done where the truck is parked, not after a haul across the valley.

Batteries

No crank on a cold morning is the common one. The lights may still glow. Write down whether the truck clicked, spun slowly, or stayed silent. A battery question is often the start of a charging question, so say if this is the first time or the third morning in a row.

Alternators

Dim lights, a battery that dies after a short drive, or a charge lamp on the dash point here. Note what was running when it happened: lights, a heater, a trailer plug. Alternator work is a mobile visit when the truck cannot be counted on to reach a shop.

Starters

A single click, a grind, or no crank at all, with lights that stay bright, is the pattern people describe. Say whether it happens every time or only when the engine is already warm. Starters are on the same card as batteries so the two are not confused in the note. Tell Jay which one you think it is, and let him confirm at the vehicle.

Cooling systems

Overheating on a grade, a sweet smell, a puddle under the front, or a temperature gauge that climbed and stayed there. Do not keep pushing a hot engine down the canyon if you can park it safely. Text Jay the location, the vehicle, and what the gauge did. Today or tomorrow is a phone call. Later windows go on the schedule, first come, first served, in Mountain Time.

Every one of these is a reason to book mobile repair and name the item. If you are on a Forest Service road with no street address, use Text Jay your location and add the nearest road or mile marker. Name one system in the note even if you are unsure, and say what the driver felt.

Name the road, the yard, or the driveway in the same note as the fault. A pin helps when the spot has no number on the curb. Call or text 435-531-4127 when the next public window is too late.
