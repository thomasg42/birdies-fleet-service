DOT inspections

DOT inspections where the trucks are parked in Southwest Montana. Call or text Jay about the trucks you need inspected. 435-531-4127.

Book a time Call 435-531-4127

Call or text Jay about the trucks you need inspected. DOT inspections sit on the Birdie’s card beside preventive maintenance, diagnosis, and emergency fleet repair. Jay Smith comes to the yard, the shop apron, the job site, or the ranch lot where the trucks already sit.

What to send

Send three facts. How many trucks. Where they are parked. Which days work. A count is enough to start, and unit numbers make the visit faster. You do not have to line the trucks up at a bay first. We come to you.

Put the count in the first line of the note. Then the place the trucks will be parked during the window. Then the days a person can meet Jay there and unlock the cabs. A lamp, a brake feel, a tire, or a steering complaint belongs in driver language. Grinding, pulling, a soft pedal, a wander, or a warning lamp are useful words. They describe what the driver felt. They are not a finished result.

Have someone on site who can start a truck if it starts. If papers should ride in the cab, ask Jay which papers to leave with the keys. Say whether each truck is loaded, empty, or still hooked. Say whether the gate locks at dusk and who can open it.

At the yard

Park each unit where the cab can open and a person can stand beside it without blocking the only lane out. If two trucks look alike, the unit number is the difference that matters. If a lamp is on, say which lamp. If the truck still drives, say whether it should be left running.

Ask Jay for a quote. Insured and locally owned describe the company. If you are still deciding between an inspection and a repair, book mobile repair or fleet maintenance and say so in the note. Jay sorts the visit when he sees the unit.

How the board works

Public booking is first come, first served and does not open for today or tomorrow. Call or text 435-531-4127 if a truck is due sooner. Fleet accounts sign in with a PIN and can ask for an earlier window. If that window belongs to someone else, the screen stops and asks before anything moves.

The towns are Bozeman, Belgrade, Manhattan, Three Forks, Ennis, Big Sky, West Yellowstone, Gardiner, Livingston, and Big Timber. Pick the town that matches the yard, then add the pin or the gate instructions so the window is not spent on the wrong approach.

Write the note the way you would talk. Count, place, days, and the one fault that keeps a truck from leaving. A long week is less useful than those lines, and the person on site should be able to stay with the units while Jay is there.

If the yard closes the gate at dusk, say who can open it during the window. If a unit is still coupled, say whether it can be dropped before Jay arrives. Those two lines keep a simple visit from turning into a second trip. Put them in the booking note with the truck count.

A fleet with several units can send the whole count in one note and still book one window, as long as the trucks will be in the same yard on the days that work. If they are scattered across two towns, say both towns and which count sits in each. Jay uses that to decide whether the visit is one stop or two.

Cold mornings, muddy lanes, and a full hook all change how long a person needs to stay with the truck. Mention them when they are true. Leave them out when they are not. The useful note is the one a driver would trust on the phone: how many trucks, where they are parked, and which days work. Call or text Jay about the trucks you need inspected.
