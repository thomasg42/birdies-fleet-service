DOT inspections

DOT inspections for fleets in Southwest Montana, done where the unit is parked. Confirm the scope with J. Call or text 435-531-4127.

Book a time Call 435-531-4127

DOT inspections are a name on the Birdies card, next to preventive maintenance, diagnosis, and emergency fleet repair. This page keeps that name and does not build a second business out of it. It does not list vehicle classes, decals, form numbers, or qualifications. Those are questions for J.

What to send

Book the visit where the unit already sits. A yard, a shop apron, a job site, or a ranch lot is enough when you write it in the note. You do not have to line the truck up at a bay first. That is the same idea as the rest of the card: we come to you.

Put the unit number first. Then the place it will be parked during the window. Then the one thing you want looked at. A lamp, a brake feel, a tire, or a steering complaint belongs in driver language. Grinding, pulling, a soft pedal, a wander, or a warning lamp are useful words. They are not a diagnosis, and they are not a result.

Have a person on site who can unlock the cab and start the truck if it starts. If papers are part of what you want reviewed, ask J which papers to leave in the cab. This page will not invent a checklist and then treat it as his.

What this page will not say

Nothing here is a rate, a package, or a promise that a visit ends with a sticker. Ask J for a quote. Insured and locally owned describe the company. They are not a stand-in for a credential this website does not have in writing.

If you are still deciding between an inspection and a repair, book mobile repair or fleet maintenance and say so in the note. J can sort the visit when he sees the unit. The website should not sort it for him, and it should not keep a paragraph that outlives the answer he gives you on the phone.

How the board works

Public booking is first come, first served and does not open for today or tomorrow. Call or text 435-531-4127 if the unit is due sooner. Fleet accounts sign in with a PIN and can ask for an earlier window. If that window belongs to someone else, the screen stops and asks before anything moves.

The towns are the same Southwest Montana list as the rest of the site: Bozeman, Belgrade, Manhattan, Three Forks, Ennis, Big Sky, West Yellowstone, Gardiner, Livingston, and Big Timber. Pick the town that matches the yard, then add the pin or the gate instructions so the window is not spent on the wrong approach.

Park the unit where the cab can open and a person can stand beside it without blocking the only lane out of the yard. Say whether the truck is loaded, empty, or still hooked. Those facts change the visit more than a story about last month. Bring the question you actually need answered, and let J say whether the look he can do matches it.

Write the note the way you would talk. Unit, place, and the fault. If two faults are fighting, mark the one that keeps the truck from leaving. A long week is less useful than those three lines, and the person on site should be able to stay with the unit while J is there.

If the yard closes the gate at dusk, say who can open it during the window. If the unit is still coupled, say whether it can be dropped before J arrives. Those two lines keep a simple visit from turning into a second trip, and they belong in the booking note rather than in a promise this page is not allowed to make.
