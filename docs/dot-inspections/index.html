DOT inspections

DOT inspections for fleets in Southwest Montana, done where the unit is parked. Scope is confirmed with J. Call or text 435-531-4127.

Book a time Call 435-531-4127

DOT inspections are on the Birdies card next to preventive maintenance, diagnosis, and emergency fleet repair. This page uses that name and stops there. Vehicle classes, inspection reports, decals, and qualifications are questions for J, not claims this website is allowed to invent.

What you can do today is book the visit. Tell J which unit, where it will be parked, and what you need him to look at. He will tell you whether that request matches the inspection he can do. If it does not, he will say so before anyone treats a web page like a certificate.

Fleets usually want the inspection where the truck already sits. A yard, a shop apron, a job site, or a ranch lot all work as the meeting place when you write them into the note. You do not have to line up a trailer first. That is the same idea as the rest of the card: we come to you.

Have the unit number ready. Have a person on site who can unlock the cab and start the truck if it starts. If paperwork is part of what you want reviewed, ask J which papers to have in the cab. This page will not guess at a form number or a checklist and then pretend it is his.

Drivers often ask for an inspection after a lamp, a brake feel, a tire, or a steering complaint. Put that complaint in the note in plain language. Grinding, pulling, a soft pedal, a wander, or a warning lamp are useful words. They are not a diagnosis. They give J a place to start when he is standing at the unit.

Public booking is first come, first served and does not open for today or tomorrow. Call or text 435-531-4127 if the unit is due sooner. Fleet accounts sign in with a PIN and can ask for an earlier window. If that window belongs to someone else, the screen stops and asks you to confirm.

Pricing is open. Ask J for a quote. Nothing on this page is a rate, a package, or a promise that an inspection ends with a particular sticker. Insured and locally owned describe the company. They are not a stand-in for a credential J has not written down here.

The towns are the same Southwest Montana list as the rest of the site: Bozeman, Belgrade, Manhattan, Three Forks, Ennis, Big Sky, West Yellowstone, Gardiner, Livingston, and Big Timber. Pick the town that matches the yard, then book the window or call.

If you are still deciding whether you need an inspection or a repair, book diagnosis or mobile repair instead, and say so in the note. J can sort the visit. The website should not sort it for him.

Write the note the way you would talk on the phone. Unit number first. Then the yard. Then the one thing the driver wants looked at. If there are two things, list both, and mark which one stops the truck from leaving. A long story about the week is less useful than those three lines. The person on site should be able to open the cab and stay with the unit while J is there.

DOT inspections stay a conversation with J about scope. This website will not add classes, decals, or a sample report to fill the page. If a fleet manager needs those answers, they belong in a call, not in a paragraph that outlives the truth. Book the window, or call 435-531-4127, and ask the question directly.

Park the unit where the cab can open and a person can stand beside it without blocking the only lane out of the yard. Tell J if the truck is loaded, empty, or still hooked to a trailer. Those three facts change the visit more than a long story about last month. Bring the question you actually need answered, and let him say whether the inspection he can do matches it.
