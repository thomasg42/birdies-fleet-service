Heavy equipment repair

Heavy equipment, construction equipment, and ranch equipment repaired on site in Southwest Montana. Ask Jay for a quote. Call 435-531-4127.

Book a time Call 435-531-4127

Heavy equipment repair covers the card items that are bigger than a pickup: heavy equipment, construction equipment, and ranch equipment. Hydraulics that live on those machines are part of the same visit. Diesel pickups stay on the diesel page so a skid steer and a crew cab are not booked as if they were the same unit.

The machine stays on the job. A loader in a pit, an excavator at a footing, a tractor in a ranch yard, or a telehandler beside a build can be the meeting place. Write the job site the way a driver would give it over the phone. A pin helps when the site is a mile of two-track past the pavement.

Say what the machine will not do. It will not start. A function is dead. A cylinder drifts. There is a leak at a fitting. The hydraulics are slow when the oil is cold. Those are notes, not a diagnosis. Jay confirms the fault at the machine. Leave the model year blank when you do not know it, and name the attachment only when you can see it.

Construction equipment often sits where the crew is not, at the end of the day. Say whether someone will be there to unlock it and to keep the site safe while the panel is open. Ranch equipment may be in a shed with the door snowed shut or in the open beside the barn. Say which.

Emergency fleet repair is the fleet page's name for a down unit. If the machine is part of a numbered fleet, a fleet account can request a sooner window. Everyone else uses the public board, which does not open today or tomorrow. Call or text Jay when the machine is stopped and the crew is waiting.

Ask Jay for a quote. The window you book is the window on the board. Insured and locally owned are the company facts.

Recovery assistance is listed under backcountry, not here. If the machine is off the road, stuck, or on a trail, use the backcountry page and text the location. If it is on a job site and simply will not run, book heavy equipment repair.

Southwest Montana job sites and ranch yards in Bozeman, Belgrade, Big Sky, Livingston, Manhattan, Three Forks, Ennis, West Yellowstone, Gardiner, and Big Timber are all on the town list. Choose the town, then choose the window, then tell Jay which machine and what it stopped doing.

Include the attachment if one is on the machine. A bucket, a fork, a spear, or a hammer changes how the panel is reached and where it is safe to stand. Say if the machine is in mud, on a slope, or tight against a building. Say if the key is in it. Those facts are ordinary and they belong in the note before the window, not after Jay is already on the site.

If the crew will be gone, name a phone that still answers. A machine with no person and no key is a wasted window. If the fault is hydraulic, describe the motion that failed: lift, curl, steer, or drive. If the fault is a no-start, say whether the lamps come on. Leave the model story blank when you are guessing.

Construction pads and ranch yards do not look alike up close. A machine against a spoil pile is not a machine in a shed. Say which, and say how close the nearest person will be. If the ground is soft, say that before the window so the visit is planned around the spot, not surprised by it.
