Diesel repair

Diesel pickups and hydraulics serviced where they sit, across Southwest Montana. J Smith comes to you. Call or text 435-531-4127.

Book a time Call 435-531-4127

Diesel repair covers diesel pickups and the hydraulic work that shows up with them. Heavy equipment, construction equipment, and ranch equipment have their own page so the two jobs are not mashed into one vague offer. J Smith comes to the pickup. The pickup does not have to come to a shop.

A diesel pickup in Southwest Montana might be the ranch truck, the contractor crew cab, or the truck that usually pulls a trailer and today will not start. Say that plainly in the note. Hard start, smoke, a miss under load, a warning lamp, or a no-crank are different visits. Name the one you have.

Cold mornings and long grades both show up in these notes. A truck that started in the yard and died on the canyon road needs a location, not a theory. If it is parked on a pullout, text a pin. If it is in a driveway in town, a normal address is enough. Use my location on the schedule page only if you want the pin in the note.

Hydraulics in this visit are the systems attached to the work the pickup is doing: a plow, a small lift, a trailer-mounted splitter, a shop crane in the yard. Leaks, a cylinder that will not hold, or a pump that howls are the details to write down. Heavy iron on a job site belongs on the heavy equipment page instead.

Diagnosis is part of fleet maintenance too. If the diesel is one unit in a numbered fleet, book fleet maintenance or sign in with the fleet PIN. If it is a single pickup, book diesel repair. Either way the fields are the same: what it is, where it sits, and a phone number.

Public windows open after tomorrow and fill first come, first served. Call or text 435-531-4127 for today or tomorrow. Ask J for a quote. There is no promise about how soon he reaches the pullout. The promise on the card is that the visit comes to you.

Towns with their own pages run from Bozeman and Belgrade out to Ennis, Big Sky, West Yellowstone, Gardiner, Livingston, and Big Timber, plus Manhattan and Three Forks. Pick the closest page if you want the local roads. The booking itself does not care which page you started on.

Fuel type matters in the note. If the truck is a diesel, say diesel. If you are not sure, say what the badge and the fuel door show. Leave the brand story out of the note. J can take it from there at the vehicle.

Add whether the truck is in a driveway, a yard, or on a pullout. Add whether it can be left in gear or needs to stay chocked because of a slope. A canyon pullout is not a flat shop floor. Those two facts keep the visit safer and shorter. If a trailer is still hooked up, say so, and say whether it can be dropped before the window.

Diesel work on a single pickup should not be booked as heavy equipment, and a skid steer should not be booked as a pickup. The pages are split so the note starts in the right place. You can still describe both in the details if a pickup and a machine are down in the same yard. J will tell you if that is one visit or two.

Write the fuel you put in it, the sound it made, and whether the fault shows up cold, hot, or only under load. A pickup that sits on a slope needs that slope mentioned. A pickup in a tight alley needs the alley mentioned. Leave brand folklore out of the note and keep the sentences a driver would trust.
